Where to Live on Saba: A Village-by-Village Guide

Published July 2026 · By Saban Realty

Saba is five square miles, and almost everyone lives in one of four villages strung along a single road. So when people ask us "where should I buy?", the honest answer is that the village matters more than the house. Homes can be renovated; the light, the breeze, the sounds, and the walk to your morning coffee come with the address. Here's how the villages actually differ.

One road, four villages

Everything on Saba hangs off The Road — the famous hand-built route that climbs from the airport at Flat Point, through Zion's Hill and Windwardside, past St. John's, down into The Bottom, and on to the harbor at Fort Bay. Nothing is far: you can drive between any two villages in a matter of minutes. What changes from village to village is elevation, orientation, and atmosphere — which side of the island wakes you with sunrise, how much of the trade-wind breeze you catch, and whether you can stroll to dinner or prefer that nobody can see your porch at all.

Windwardside — the island's living room

Windwardside is Saba's social heart: the shops, most of the restaurants, the dive operators, and the trailhead up Mount Scenery are all here, wrapped in the storybook cluster of white cottages, green shutters, and red roofs the island is known for. If you want to walk to coffee, bump into friends, and be where things happen, this is the village.

It's also where the market is deepest. Current Windwardside listings run from CatsEye Cottage, a restored two-bedroom in the village at $425,000, up through character homes like House on the Path and the multi-unit Anslyn Estate, to view-led villas approaching $1M. Most of the island's vacation rentals cluster here too, which matters if you're weighing rental income.

Booby Hill — the ridge above it all

Just above Windwardside, the hamlet of Booby Hill trades walkability for privacy and panorama. Homes up here sit on the ridge with sweeping views over the village and the Caribbean Sea, a few minutes' drive from town rather than a stroll. It's long been one of the island's most coveted addresses — think properties like Iguana Rock House, a solar-powered villa with cathedral ceilings at $890,000. If your picture of island life is a quiet terrace and a big horizon, start here.

The Bottom — the capital in the green bowl

Despite the name, The Bottom sits in a high valley ringed by green slopes — it's the seat of government, home to the hospital, and host to the Saba University School of Medicine. That last point is worth underlining for buyers: the steady flow of students and faculty makes The Bottom the strongest village for long-term rental demand, a theme we cover in our look at Saba as an investment. It's also the closest village to Fort Bay, where the dive boats and the ferry leave from.

On the market side, current options include the Troy Hill Residence at $550,000 and building land along Wells Bay Road from around $106,000.

St. John's — the quiet in-between

Between Windwardside and The Bottom, St. John's is the smallest of the four villages — a hillside cluster of homes with the island's schools and a spectacular open view across the channel to St. Eustatius. It's the natural fit for families with school-age children and for anyone who wants a real neighborhood feel without the comings and goings of the busier villages. Fair warning: homes here reach the market rarely, so patience — and telling us what you're looking for in advance — goes a long way.

Zion's Hill — the sunrise side

Zion's Hill (you'll also hear its traditional name, Hell's Gate) is the first village The Road reaches from the airport, terraced into the island's northeastern slopes. It gets the morning sun, keeps a quieter, more local rhythm, and puts you closest to the tide pools at Cove Bay and to your flight out. Our own office is here, so we're admittedly fond of it.

It's also the best-stocked village for land: current parcels range from about $99,000 to $209,000 — a real path to building exactly the house you want. Built options run from The Mill, a landmark villa at $775,000, to Seagrape Cottage at Cove Bay, one of the island's rare oceanfront homes.

The quick chooser: walk-to-dinner village life → Windwardside. Privacy and the biggest views → Booby Hill. Rental demand, the med school, and the harbor → The Bottom. Schools and quiet → St. John's. Morning sun, land to build, and the airport five minutes away → Zion's Hill.

Choose the village, then the house

Our best advice for a first visit: spend a morning and an evening in each village before you fall for a listing. Light and breeze change through the day, and most buyers discover a strong preference within forty-eight hours of walking The Road. When you're ready, browse what's currently for sale — every listing notes its village — or read our step-by-step buying guide and cost-of-living breakdown to round out the picture.
