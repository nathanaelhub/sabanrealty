Statia Day 2026: 250 Years Since the First Salute

Published November 2026 · By Saban Realty

Every 16 November, St. Eustatius celebrates Statia Day. This year is a big one: 2026 marks 250 years since the First Salute, the morning a small Dutch island became the first place in the world to acknowledge the flag of the United States. Here is what happened, what is planned, and why it is a good moment to see our neighbour island for yourself.

What happened on 16 November 1776

Four months after the Declaration of Independence, the American brig Andrew Doria, under Captain Isaiah Robinson, sailed into the anchorage below Fort Oranje flying the Continental Colors. She announced herself with a thirteen-gun salute, one for each of the colonies in rebellion.

The island's governor, Johannes de Graaff, had the fort reply with eleven guns. By the custom of the day, answering with two guns fewer was how a fort acknowledged a sovereign flag. It was the first time a foreign power had done so for the United States, and it is remembered on both sides of the Atlantic as the First Salute.

Why it happened here

In the eighteenth century Statia was one of the busiest trading ports on earth, a free port nicknamed the Golden Rock. Hundreds of ships at a time lay at anchor off Oranjestad, and the Andrew Doria had come to buy supplies for the American cause. You can still read that history in the town: the warehouse ruins along the Lower Town shore, the cobbled path up the cliff, and Fort Oranje itself looking out over the bay.

What came after

The salute did not go unnoticed. Britain protested, and in 1781 Admiral George Rodney seized the island and its warehouses, a blow from which the Golden Rock's trade never fully recovered. The friendship outlasted the damage. In 1939 President Franklin D. Roosevelt presented a plaque that still hangs at Fort Oranje, thanking the island for the salute, and Statia's airport carries his name today.

What is planned for the 250th anniversary

The commemoration centres on Fort Oranje. A celebration at the fort is scheduled for the evening of Sunday 15 November, and the main ceremony takes place on the morning of Monday 16 November. The Dutch government has announced that King Willem-Alexander will attend, together with Prime Minister Rob Jetten and the State Secretary for Kingdom Relations. Delegations from the United States are expected as well.

Around the official programme, Statia Day brings parades, music and food to the streets of Oranjestad. Details change, so check the island government's and tourism office's announcements for the final schedule before you travel.

At a glance: Statia Day is 16 November · 2026 is the 250th anniversary of the First Salute · ceremony at Fort Oranje on the morning of the 16th · the island is about twenty minutes by air from Sint Maarten.

Visiting Statia for the anniversary

Fly to Sint Maarten (SXM), then take the Winair flight to F.D. Roosevelt Airport, around twenty minutes over open water. The island is small and rooms are limited, so for the anniversary week book flights and accommodation as early as you can.

Beyond the ceremonies, give yourself time for the things Statia does quietly well: the hike up The Quill, whose crater holds a rainforest; the walk between Upper Town and Lower Town; and diving in a protected marine park with reefs and eighteenth-century wrecks. A morning in Oranjestad

If you only have a few hours, start at Fort Oranje, where the cannons still face the bay and the plaque is easy to find. From there the Upper Town is a short stroll of old stone and wooden houses, church ruins and the island's historical museum. Take the cobbled Old Slave Road down the cliff to the Lower Town, where the foundations of the old warehouses run along the shore and, in places, out under the water. It is a small town, and that is the point: you can walk two and a half centuries of history before lunch.

What the attention means for the island

For most of the past century Statia has been overlooked, which is a large part of why it is still unhurried and still itself. A royal visit and international coverage will put it in front of people who have never heard of it. We don't expect that to change the island's character, and we would not want it to. It may, though, bring a few more visitors who look around and wonder what it would be like to stay.

If that turns out to be you, the property market is small and straightforward. Like Saba, Statia is a special municipality of the Netherlands, there are no nationality restrictions on ownership, and prices are in US dollars.
